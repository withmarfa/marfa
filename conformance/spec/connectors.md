# Connectors

The smallest doors a process outside the server needs: it registers with a name and a description under the key it holds, heartbeats, reports each run with its outcome, holds its registration so that only the process holding it replaces its state or writes its agreements, and keeps what it needs to resume on the instance rather than beside itself; the server lists what registered. No runtime, no supervision, no manifests: the server starts, watches and restarts nothing, and a hold is a lock a process takes, renews and gives up, which lapses when the process stops renewing it rather than because anything noticed. A reader decides what a stale heartbeat or a failed run means.

## Registration

1. `POST /connectors` with `{name, description?}` registers the caller's key as a connector and answers `201` with `{id, key_id, source, name, description, registered_at, updated_at, last_heartbeat_at, last_run, hold_expires_at}`, `source` being the key's; `last_heartbeat_at`, `last_run` and `hold_expires_at` are `null` until a heartbeat, a run or a hold arrives. A name outside 1 to 200 characters, or a description over 2000, answers `400 validation_error`. `compliance/connectors.test.ts › registers the key as a connector and lists it`, `› refuses a name or a description outside the bounds`.
2. One registration per key: the same key registering again updates the name and the description and answers `200` with the same `id`, its `updated_at` moved and its `registered_at` kept. `compliance/connectors.test.ts › registers once per key, updating on repeat`.
3. `GET /connectors` lists every registration to any key, the newest registration first, each with its `key_id`, the key's `source`, its `last_heartbeat_at`, its `last_run` and its `hold_expires_at` (statement 12); `GET /connectors/{id}` answers one, and an id no registration carries answers `404 connector_not_found` on it and on every door under it. `compliance/connectors.test.ts › registers the key as a connector and lists it`, `› answers 404 for an unknown connector, where a registered one answers`.
4. `DELETE /connectors/{id}` removes the registration, its runs, and its inbound webhook endpoints with every delivery they stored (`inbound-webhooks.md` 4), to the connector's own key or the operator key; another key is refused `403 forbidden` and the registration stands. What the connector kept on the instance stays with its source (statement 16). `compliance/connectors.test.ts › removes a registration for its own key or the operator, never another`.
5. A registration outlives its key: revoking the key leaves the registration and its runs listed under the `key_id` they were written with, and the key's heartbeats answer `401 unauthorized` from then on, until the operator removes it. Nothing supervises a registration whose process can no longer reach it; what a stale heartbeat means is the reader's to decide. `compliance/connectors.test.ts › keeps a registration whose key was revoked, until the operator removes it`.

A session token an app holds is not a key: `POST /connectors` refuses it `403 forbidden`, because the token is renewed on every refresh and a registration keyed to one would be orphaned by the next, and the doors that admit the connector's own key alone refuse it the same way, the clearing door of statement 22 among them. The referee mints no session token, so it is not a statement here; `packages/server/src/routes/connectors.test.ts` proves both, beside the same token reading the listing, and `routes/connector-state.test.ts` proves it on every door of statements 12 to 22. `routes/connectors.test.ts` also proves that a key minted later under a revoked key's `source` is another key to statement 5's registration: a source is unique among live keys only, and the registration is the key's.

## Heartbeats and runs

6. `POST /connectors/{id}/heartbeat` from the connector's own key answers `200 {last_heartbeat_at}`, the server's clock, and the listing carries it; another key, the operator's included, is refused `403 forbidden` and the stamp does not move. `compliance/connectors.test.ts › takes a heartbeat from the connector's key alone`.
7. `POST /connectors/{id}/runs` with `{outcome, started_at, finished_at, summary?, error?}` from the connector's own key records a run and answers `201` with it, `reported_at` being the server's clock; `outcome` is `succeeded` or `failed` and anything else answers `400 validation_error`, as do a `started_at` or `finished_at` that is not a timestamp, a `finished_at` before `started_at`, and a `summary` or `error` over 2000 characters; another key, the operator's included, is refused `403 forbidden`. `compliance/connectors.test.ts › records a run from the connector's key and refuses an outcome it does not know`.
8. `GET /connectors/{id}/runs` lists a connector's own runs to any key, newest by `reported_at` first, `limit` at most 200 and 50 unless given, a `limit` outside that answering `400 validation_error`, and a page the `limit` cut answering a `next_cursor` that reaches the rest; the listing's `last_run` is the connector's newest by the same order. `compliance/connectors.test.ts › lists runs newest first and names the last on the registration`, `› keeps the last hundred runs`.
9. The server keeps the last 100 runs per connector: the 101st reported drops the oldest. `compliance/connectors.test.ts › keeps the last hundred runs`.
10. Every registration, first or repeat, and every removal write audit rows (`connector.register` carrying whether it created the row, `connector.delete`); heartbeats and runs do not. `compliance/connectors.test.ts › audits every registration and a removal, not a heartbeat or a run`.
11. `GET /` names `connectors` among its features. `compliance/instance.test.ts › describes itself at the root`.

## The hold

12. `POST /connectors/{id}/hold` with `{process}`, a string of 1 to 100 characters the process names itself by, from the connector's own key, takes the registration's hold for that process, or renews it when the process already holds it, and answers `200 {expires_at, renewed}`. `expires_at` is the server's clock plus the instance's hold window, three minutes unless `MARFA_CONNECTOR_HOLD_MS` names another. `renewed` is `true` only when this process's hold was still live when the call arrived, and `false` on a first take and on any take after its hold lapsed, whether or not another process held the registration in between; the lapse is asserted against a server booted with a short window. A process answered `renewed: false` while it believed it held the registration may have been displaced, so it re-reads the state and the agreements before writing again. The registration's `hold_expires_at` in the listing and on its own read is `expires_at` while the hold is live, and `null` when nothing holds it or the hold has lapsed. A hold stamps no heartbeat, and a heartbeat takes no hold. A `process` outside its bounds answers `400 validation_error`, and a body naming none `400 missing_required_field`. `compliance/connector-state.test.ts › takes and renews the hold for one process, and shows it on the registration`, `› refuses a process outside its bounds`, `› tells a process whose hold lapsed that it did not renew it`.
13. While another process holds a live hold, taking it answers `409 connector_held` with that hold's `expires_at` in `details.expires_at`, and the hold does not move. Once `expires_at` has passed, any process takes it; that is asserted against a server booted with a short window. `compliance/connector-state.test.ts › refuses a second process while the hold is live`, `› lets another process take a hold that lapsed, and takes its writes once it holds it`.
14. `DELETE /connectors/{id}/hold?process=` releases the hold if that process holds it and answers `200 {ok: true}`. Releasing a hold the process does not hold, or one nothing holds, answers the same and leaves any other process's hold standing. A `process` outside its bounds answers `400 validation_error`, and a request naming none `400 missing_required_field`. `compliance/connector-state.test.ts › releases the hold for its holder alone, and answers the same when there is nothing to release`, `› refuses a process outside its bounds`.
15. Only the connector's own key takes or releases its hold: another key and the operator key are refused `403 forbidden`, and the hold does not move. `compliance/connector-state.test.ts › holds for the connector's own key alone`.

## What a connector keeps on the instance

16. A connector keeps a state document and its agreements on the instance, and both belong to its registration's `source` rather than to the registration or its key. A connector of another source neither reads nor clears them, though it keeps its own record of the same row. Removing the registration leaves them, and a key minted later with the same `source`, once the first is revoked, registers and reads the same state and the same agreements, whether or not the first registration still stands. Nothing sweeps what a removed registration leaves when no other registration of its source stands: to clear it, register a key of that `source` and clear through that registration (statement 22). `compliance/connector-state.test.ts › keeps each source's state and agreements apart`, `› hands the state and the agreements to the next key with the same source`, `› clears what a removed registration left, through a later registration of its source`.
17. `GET /connectors/{id}/state` answers `{state, updated_at}`, `{}` and `null` until anything is written. `PUT /connectors/{id}/state` with `{process, state}`, `state` a JSON object, replaces the document whole and answers it, `updated_at` the server's clock, when `process` holds the registration (statement 21). A `state` over 512 KiB serialized answers `400 validation_error` and writes nothing, and one at 512 KiB is taken; a `state` that is not an object answers `400 validation_error`, and a body missing `process` or `state` answers `400 missing_required_field`. `compliance/connector-state.test.ts › reads an empty state, and replaces it whole`, `› refuses a state over its cap and takes one at it`.
18. An agreement is the connector's record of what it and its vendor last agreed about one row, one per row for the source. `POST /connectors/{id}/agreements` with `{process, set?, clear?}` writes them when `process` holds the registration (statement 21): each entry of `set`, at most 500, is `{item_id, waiting, record}`, `waiting` a boolean and `record` a JSON object of at most 16 KiB serialized, and replaces the row's record; each id in `clear`, at most 500, removes the row's record. It answers `200 {written, cleared, skipped}`: an id that names no stored row, or a row whose type the key's type map does not read, is skipped and named in `skipped` in the order named, rather than refusing the batch, and a trashed row is a stored row. A list over 500, a record over its cap or not an object, a `waiting` that is not a boolean, or a row named twice across the two lists answers `400 validation_error`, as does a top-level body field the door does not declare, and a missing `process` or an entry missing a field answers `400 missing_required_field`; either writes nothing. `compliance/connector-state.test.ts › writes and clears agreements, skipping an id it cannot hold`, `› refuses a batch over its caps and writes nothing`.
19. `POST /connectors/{id}/agreements/find` with `{item_ids}`, 1 to 500 of them, answers `{data}` with the agreement of each named row that has one, in the order named, as `{item_id, waiting, record, updated_at}`, and no cursor. `GET /connectors/{id}/agreements` lists the source's agreements, the one written longest ago first, only the waiting ones under `waiting=true` and only the others under `waiting=false`, a `limit` outside 1 to 200 or a `waiting` that is neither answering `400 validation_error`, a page the limit cut answering a `next_cursor` that reaches the rest, and a query key it does not declare refused (`search-and-filters.md` 8). A row whose type the key's type map does not read is left out of both, as it is skipped by a clear, so a page of the listing can be short with a cursor still to follow; its record stays, and is read again once the key reads the type. `compliance/connector-state.test.ts › finds agreements by row and lists the waiting ones a page at a time`, `› writes and clears agreements, skipping an id it cannot hold`, `› refuses a batch over its caps and writes nothing`, `› leaves a row the key no longer reads out of its reads and its clears`.
20. Writing an agreement announces nothing on the event stream and leaves its row as it was, the row's `updated_at` and `version` unmoved, where an ordinary update to the same row moves both and is announced. Purging a row removes its agreements. The fixture watches the row, because other files write to the same server; `packages/server/src/routes/connector-state.test.ts` watches the whole stream across the write and sees nothing. `compliance/connector-state.test.ts › writes an agreement without touching the row or announcing it`, `› goes with a purged row`.
21. `PUT /connectors/{id}/state` and `POST /connectors/{id}/agreements` are taken only from the `process` that holds a live hold on the registration. From any other process they answer `409 connector_held` and write nothing: with the live hold's `expires_at` in `details.expires_at` while another process holds it, and with no `details.expires_at` when no process holds a live hold, whether none was taken, it was released or it lapsed. So a process whose hold lapsed writes nothing more until its next take, which answers `409 connector_held` while another process holds the registration and `renewed: false` once none does (statement 12), and it re-reads the state and the agreements before writing again. The clear of statement 22 is not fenced. `compliance/connector-state.test.ts › fences the state and the agreements to the process that holds the registration`, `› lets another process take a hold that lapsed, and takes its writes once it holds it`, `› refuses a write from a process whose hold lapsed, whether its successor released the hold or let it lapse`, `› tells a process whose hold lapsed that it did not renew it`.
22. Only the connector's own key reads or writes its state and its agreements: another key and the operator key are refused `403 forbidden` on each of these doors, and nothing is written. `DELETE /connectors/{id}/state` is the one door the operator key reaches too: from either, it removes the source's state document and every agreement, answers `200 {ok: true}`, and writes a `connector_state.clear` audit row against the registration named, carrying the source, whether a state document went and how many agreements; another key is refused `403 forbidden` and nothing goes. The operator's clear through any registration of a source, a standing one whose key was revoked included, takes that source's whole store, a successor's live state among it, and no hold fences it. `compliance/connector-state.test.ts › keeps state and agreements to the connector's own key`, `› clears the state and the agreements for the own key or the operator, and audits it`, `› hands the state and the agreements to the next key with the same source`.
